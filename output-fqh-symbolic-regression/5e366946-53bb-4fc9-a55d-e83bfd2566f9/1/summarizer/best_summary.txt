## 3. Strategic Analysis (The "So What?")
- **Root Cause:** Predominantly the **quality of the plan**, executed faithfully. The planner's structural diagnosis ("the parent contains no correct physics; vertical optimization is useless") was the decisive factor. The executor delivered exactly the intended structure, and the least-squares fit converged to physically sensible values — p1 = 0.2807 sits near the analytic Laughlin anchor 1/4 (0.25), confirming the envelope is genuine, not fitted noise. Execution was competent (23 optimizer evaluations, xtol termination, monotone-safe from the p2=p3=0 start), but it was not the source of the leap.
- **Key Insight:** **Structure & symmetry sector dominate error, not numerical tuning.** ~99.9% of the fidelity gain came from fixing angular momentum (M=1 → M=3, i.e., the odd exponent 3) and adding the Gaussian envelope; the surviving LL-mixing corrections (p2, p3) are tiny (|p2|≈0.014, p3≈0.038) and contribute only a marginal sharpening of the residual NMSE. The lesson: in this search, identifying the correct symmetry sector and dominant factorized core is worth orders of magnitude more than parameter refinement — a strategy that should be reused for any future exponential/structure jumps. A valuable secondary concept to salvage: the `|p1|, |p2|` magnitude-wrapping trick guarantees finiteness for *all* fitted values, making candidates "monotonically safe" (fit can only add corrections that lower NMSE).
- **Identified Risk:** Overfitting the tiny residual. The nonzero p2, p3 may be fitting training/validation noise rather than genuine Landau-level mixing; the complexity penalty is already biting (0.99452), and the accuracy term is saturating (0.99919). Pushing complexity upward for a ~1e-3 NMSE improvement risks hidden-test degradation and erosion of the score through penalty growth. Secondary risk: assumed M=3 holomorphic Laughlin structure is a strong prior — the task explicitly warns the target is NOT necessarily LLL-projected, so the near-perfect fidelity here could partly reflect the data's dominant Laughlin component while missing genuine small non-holomorphic corrections.
