======================================================================
PLAN 3  (ALTERNATIVE, LEAST ROBUST)
Open-ended symbolic-regression / genetic-programming search over
non-holomorphic structures with a constraint-repair operator
======================================================================

1. MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Variables  X = the expression TREE itself (structure) + its numeric leaves.
   Primitive set: {z1, z2, conj(z1), conj(z2), +, -, *, /, **2, **3,
   exp, abs, add/mul by fitted complex constants}.
Constraints C:
   C1 fermionic antisymmetry, C2 single integer M,
   C3 finiteness on the sampled domain, C4 <=10 fitted parameters,
   C5 no memorization / no I/O (hard task rules).
Objective f(X): multi-objective
   maximize Score = accuracy_component * physics_factor * complexity_penalty,
   i.e. minimize validation NMSE with penalties for exchange_error,
   rotation_error, non-finite fraction, and expression complexity.

2. SOLVER STRATEGY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
   Genetic programming (tree crossover + subtree mutation) over the
   primitive set, with a repair operator applied to every offspring:
      repair(tree): find the antisymmetric relative factor; if none,
        multiply by a minimal (z_rel)^(odd) factor to restore C1;
        then symmetrize rotation content to the nearest integer M;
        then clamp any exponential argument to be <=0 (finiteness).
   Numeric leaves refit by scipy.optimize.least_squares after each edit.

3. VALIDITY MECHANISM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
   The constraint-repair operator is applied BEFORE evaluation, so every
   evaluated individual is feasible by construction. A final assertion
   re-checks C1-C3 numerically; infeasible results are discarded.

4. WHY THIS SOLUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
   Advantages:
     (+) Maximum structural freedom; could discover surprises (e.g. a
         different M sector or an unexpected Jastrow form).
   Disadvantages:
     (-) Expensive, high variance, hard to reproduce -> contradicts the
         "reproducible across independent runs" success criterion.
     (-) Large overfitting and complexity-penalty risk.
     (-) Slower to yield a first good candidate; only recommended after
         the structured families (Plans 1-2) have visibly plateaued.
======================================================================
