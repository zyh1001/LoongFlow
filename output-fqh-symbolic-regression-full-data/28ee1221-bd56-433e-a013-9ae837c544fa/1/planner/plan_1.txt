      d_ij = z_i - z_j ,  i < j

      S    = sum_i |z_i|^2                           (rotational invariant)
      G    = |sum_i z_i|^2                           (CM rotational invariant)

CANDIDATE FAMILY
      Psi(z; alpha, gamma) = J^m * exp( -alpha*S - gamma*G )

HARD CONSTRAINTS  C  (must hold for EVERY candidate)
  C1  ANTISYMMETRY: exchange of electrons i<->j sends d_ij -> -d_ij, i.e. J -> -J.
      With m odd, J^m -> -J^m, and the real envelope is exchange invariant, so
      Psi(...,r_i,...,r_j,...) = -Psi(...,r_j,...,r_i,...).      [constructive]
  C2  SINGLE ANGULAR-MOMENTUM SECTOR: z_i -> e^{-i phi} z_i  gives
      J^m -> e^{-i phi * m * N(N-1)/2} J^m = e^{-i phi * m * 15} J^m; both S and G
      are invariant, therefore Psi belongs to the single sector
      M = m * N(N-1)/2 = m * 15  (for m = 3 => M = 45).           [constructive]
  C3  FINITENESS: for alpha > 0 the Gaussian decays and J^m is a polynomial; the
      expression is finite for every finite coordinate sample (finite_fraction=1).
  C4  REPRESENTATION LEGALITY: the exponent m is an integer structural literal
      (|m| <= 12); the invariants are built from integer powers and np.abs;
      NO decimal literal appears in the return expression; no parameter encodes
      only a global phase / global scale.

OBJECTIVE FUNCTION
  maximize   accuracy = fidelity * finite_fraction,
             fidelity = |<Psi|Psi_target>|^2 / ( ||Psi||^2 * ||Psi_target||^2 )
  on the uniformly sampled 50000-sample full pool.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHY THIS SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Expected improvement: the parent omits BOTH the correct Jastrow power and the
Gaussian envelope, so its overlap with a nu = 1/3 state is ~1e-6.  Restoring the
m = 3 power and the LLL Gaussian should raise fidelity from ~3e-6 towards unity.
Advantages: analytically valid (antisymmetry + single M sector by construction),
lowest complexity of the three outlines, physically interpretable, perfectly
reproducible.
Disadvantages: the leading ansatz is exact only in the LLL; strong LL mixing
(kappa = 100) may leave a residual gap, which is handled by the fitted gamma term
and, if needed, by the alternate plans 2 and 3.
